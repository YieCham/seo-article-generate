# Explainer voice — corpus notes

Source folder: `文风学习/`. All four pieces are Wait But Why (Tim Urban). Stats are from a simple sentence split (markdown links stripped). Treat as targets, not laws.

## Sentence stats

| Piece | n | mean | median | ≤8 words | 9–20 | ≥21 | long→short punches |
|-------|---|------|--------|----------|------|-----|--------------------|
| A Short History of My Last Six Years | 174 | 14.9 | 13 | 40% | 33% | 28% | 15 |
| You Won't Believe My Morning | 237 | 16.4 | 14 | 27% | 42% | 30% | 16 |
| Tales from Toddlerhood | 61 | 21.5 | 19 | 21% | 36% | 43% | 4 |
| The sights and sounds of Bhutan | 33 | 21.1 | 20 | 18% | 33% | 49% | 2 |

Narrative / dialogue (Six Years, Morning) runs shorter and punches more. Expository travel and listed observations run longer, then still drop a short line (*Anyway, gotta go.*).

Nominalization hits in the corpus: essentially none (`the fact that` ×1, `the process of` ×1). Passive shows up for history, hiring, and metaphor (*being trampled*), not for "the action is performed by X".

Punch closes (last sentence of a paragraph ≤8 words): ~45–50% in the two narrative pieces; much lower in Bhutan's scene-setting.

## Verb-driven vs frozen nouns

Urban writes actions:

- *I opened WordPress to write a little post… But nothing came out.*
- *We dusted off The List, picked a country… tossed the kids to the grandparents…*
- *you find your way onto one of these giants' football-field-size teeth, and you flick the grain of sand into his kilometer-wide chasm of a mouth.*

SEO rewrite pair:

- Frozen: *The utilization of a dedicated repair workflow is recommended following the completion of official troubleshooting.*
- Driven: *If Apple's steps didn't get you unstuck, use a repair tool next.*

## Metaphor catalog (what to steal)

| Move | Example | Why it works |
|------|---------|--------------|
| Scale ratio | virus : sand :: sand : house | Two jumps of 10,000; reader can feel both ends |
| Litmus thought experiment | flying elephant / cartoon hello | Tests a mental state without defining it |
| Job title for a behavior | toddlers as mid-century dictators | One image carries the whole section |
| Weather for mood | *a cloud hanging over all these topics* | Felt before it is analyzed |
| Quest object | The List; the Society folder; trans-tier station | Gives the abstract a physical handle |
| Rejected pop analogy | "Kind of like Inception?" / "Not really." | Shows the limit of the metaphor; earns trust |

Do not copy the shrinking-village plot into a product tutorial. Copy the **ratio + familiar object** method.

## Persona tells

- Dates and inventories: *June 18, 2016. Obama was president… I'm 41.*
- Self-roast: *The delusion of a madman. I have problems. Somewhere, a fairy died.*
- Calibrated doubt: *which I had no way to verify, but the people were very kind and seemed pretty happy I guess?*
- Aside in parentheses, then back to the point
- Profanity as honesty, not decoration (drop this in `seo` mode)

## Structural skeletons (full pieces)

### Six Years — escalating chronicle

1. **Hook:** time-capsule sentence + dramatic irony (*whole life ahead of me*).
2. **False start:** meant to write a mini-post; fear is part of the topic.
3. **Stakes inflate:** notes → folder → wedding deadline missed → series → Chapter 11 explodes → book → pregnancy as real deadline.
4. **Socratic beats:** *Was it social media? Politics? A broader phenomenon?*
5. **Close:** practical handoff (formats, free code) + *Can't wait to hear your thoughts.* Not a proverb; a baton pass.

Use for origin stories, "why this tool exists", long troubleshooting paths that got worse before they got better.

### Toddlerhood — paradox list

1. **Hook:** callback + *I'm not here to talk about X.*
2. Each H2 is both/and (*obsessed and bored*; *geniuses who are also very dumb*).
3. Proof is one anecdote, then a short verdict.
4. **Close:** walk off into ordinary life (*57th time*).

Use for myth-busting explainers (*You can be X and still Y*).

### Bhutan — list as compass (opening only in corpus)

1. **Hook:** a personal system (The List) + mortality (*we remembered that we were gonna die*).
2. Staccato names (*Ethiopia. Indonesia. South Korea.*) then one long establishing block.
3. Character of a place via **two king stories** (tour workers; stray dogs), not a Wikipedia dump.
4. Opens toward "why it was on The List" — circular.

Use for "what this category actually is" sections: two vivid stories > ten adjectives.

### Morning — absurdist thought experiment

1. **Hook:** clickbait title immediately deflated by a dumb daily ritual, then the glint.
2. Dialogue as Socratic ladder: each answer (size → time → virus → lower tier) raises the question.
3. Controlling metaphor = scale; pop-culture analogy is offered and refused.
4. **Close:** unanswered *How many tiers?* / *One thing at a time.*

Use when you must make a scale, risk, or invisible system feel physical. Not for step-by-step UI copy.

## Hook / close cheat sheet for SEO parts

**Introduction (≤3 paragraphs)**

1. Scene, number, experiment, or Not-X (one only).
2. One sentence naming the real confusion.
3. Map of what the parts will unpack — no product pitch.

**Generic Part**

- Open with the misconception the last section left alive.
- Teach the mechanism with verbs + optional scale metaphor.
- End on a short line the next Part can push against.

**Product Part**

- Transition: official path stalled; here is a narrower tool.
- Keep the same metaphor (*unpack the damaged layer*).
- Steps stay imperative verbs. No origin-myth recap.

**Conclusion**

- One punch line the reader could quote.
- One next action or open question.
- If a CTA is required, attach it to the punch, don't replace the punch.
